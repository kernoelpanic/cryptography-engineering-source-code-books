Diffie–Hellman Toy Demo

Two entities establish the same secret number while everything they say out loud stays useless to an eavesdropper. Therefore, they use a “clock” with 17 positions and a hand that hops.

The metaphor

Everything below happens on a clock: numbers that grow to large wrap around.

p The clock face

The clock has 17 positions, 0…16. Counting past the top wraps back to 0. This resembles computation “mod p”.

g One hop

A hop multiplies the hand's position by 3, then wraps. Starting at 1: 1 → 3 → 9 → 10 → 13 (all mod p). The exponent defines the number of hops. The base is called the generator.

? The trapdoor

Hopping forward is easy. Looking at where the hand stopped and saying how many hops it took is the discrete logarithm problem — no shortcut known.

Why this is hard for Eve

Eve has the whole conversation: p, g, A and B. To get the key she needs a (or b) i.e., the hop count hidden behind A. In case of this toy example she can just try them all.

Changes to real Diffie-Hellman

  • (Sub)-Group Properties: The algebraic group which is used for DH has to fulfill some properties for Dlog to be considered hard (e.g., order of the group has to be prime).
  • Parameter Sizes:>Real Diffie–Hellman uses a prime p with 2048 to 4096 bits for finite‑field DH (FFDH) and g is usually just 2. Same picture, unimaginably bigger clock (approx. 10616 entries).
  • Ellipitc Curves: Besides FFDH there is also ECDH over the algebraic structure of elliptic curves. The clock becomes an elliptic curve, hopping becomes point addition, and keys are 32 bytes (for example X25519 used in TLS, Signal, SSH, ...).
  • Fresh public key per session: Ephemeral DH keys give forward secrecy: a stolen long‑term key cannot decrypt yesterday's traffic.
  • Shared secret number is not used directly: The shared secret number goes through a KDF (e.g. HKDF) before it is used as an encryption key for a symmetric scheme such as some mode of AES.
  • No active attack protection: Plain DH is MITM‑able — it gives you a secret, not proof of whose secret. Signatures or certificates required for that part.
